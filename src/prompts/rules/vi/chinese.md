# Chinese → Vietnamese — Language-Specific Rules

## Names
- Translate character names to Hán Việt when appropriate (e.g., 李明 → Lý Minh, 陆远秋 → Lục Viễn Thu)
- For Western-derived or invented fantasy names transcribed in Chinese, use the canonical or context-supported Latin spelling instead of Hán Việt (e.g., 凯尔 → Kyle)
- Prefer glossary or official spelling; when the spelling is ambiguous, choose one plausible form and keep it consistent without claiming unsupported certainty
- Place names: use Hán Việt (e.g., 青云山 → Thanh Vân Sơn)

## Pronouns (context-dependent)
- 我 → tôi / ta / mình (depends on character's personality and scene)
- 你 → cậu / ngươi / anh / em (depends on relationship)
- 他/她 → hắn / y / anh ấy / cô ấy (depends on tone)

## Honorifics
- 前辈 → tiền bối
- 师父 → sư phụ
- 大人 → đại nhân
- 兄弟 → huynh đệ
- 老师 → thầy / cô (depends on gender)

## Narrative Style
- Preserve shifts between narration, dialogue, internal thought, and quoted messages without expanding compact Chinese phrasing into explanations
- Resolve omitted subjects and references from context, but do not make implicit motives or relationships explicit
- When humor is present, preserve punchline timing, dry irony, wordplay, and face or status dynamics by function; do not explain, intensify, or invent jokes
