# Common Translation Rules (All Languages -> English)

## English Prose
- Write idiomatic English while preserving the source register: formal, casual, archaic, comic, or intimate.
- Action: maintain momentum and clear spatial flow; use short clauses only where the source rhythm supports them.
- Romance/drama: preserve subtext, hesitation, silence, and tension without explaining or embellishing them.
- Comedy: preserve timing, contrast, wordplay, and punchlines by function rather than literal syntax.
- Exposition: prioritize clarity without sounding more academic than the source.
- Internal monologue: preserve the character's rhythm, bias, uncertainty, and emotional movement.
- Avoid stiff calques, source-language word order, repetitive sentence frames, and unnecessary discourse markers.

## Dialogue
- Use natural English punctuation and paragraphing while preserving meaningful pauses, interruptions, ellipses, and exclamations.
- Match diction and contractions to the speaker's age, status, personality, relationship, and register.
- Do not add dialogue tags, names, or pronouns where the source leaves them implicit and English remains clear.

## Relationship, Address, and Voice
- Preserve social distance and hierarchy through diction, titles, and formality rather than flattening every relationship into first-name address.
- Use a title only when it fits the setting; otherwise render its social function naturally.
- Keep each character's naming and reference style consistent with the glossary and current relationship.

## Names, Slang, and Presentation
- Use the glossary for established names; otherwise choose one readable rendering or romanization and keep it consistent.
- Translate idioms, jokes, internet slang, and sound effects by function and register; retain a source term only when established or contextually clear.
- Preserve recognizable in-world formats for system windows, status screens, posts, messages, and chat logs.
- Preserve meaningful source-specific symbols and brackets such as 【】, 『』, and 「」.
